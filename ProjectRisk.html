Sensitivity: an e-scooter fleet

Move one input at a time and watch the NPV and the break-even points (slides 8 to 11). The tornado chart ranks the inputs for the change you choose.

The case ($m, fictional). A mobility company is deciding whether to launch an e-scooter fleet in a new city. Fleet and depot cost $6m now, depreciated straight line over 3 years. Base case: 4.0 million rides a year at $3.00 a ride; variable cost (charging, repairs) $1.40 a ride; fixed cash costs (depot, staff, city licence) $2m a year. Tax 25%; discount rate 12%. Base-case NPV: $3.13m.

Option to abandon the fleet

After one year the company knows which scenario it is in. It can keep running the e-scooters or sell the fleet to another operator. Change the sale value and see when the option is worth having (slide 33).

Book value at the end of year 1 is $4m; tax of 25% is paid on any gain over book value, or saved on a loss. Scenarios: best 5.0m rides at $3.20 (25%), base (50%), worst 2.8m rides at $2.60 (25%).

Value of the option to abandon against the sale value. Below the kink nobody sells; above it, a higher exit price protects more of the downside.

Mini case: a vertical farm

Work the risk analysis from start to finish. Check each step as you go; the solution for a step appears once you ask for it. Use $m, and $ thousand a tonne for prices.

The analyst's file ($m, fictional)

  • A food company is deciding whether to build an indoor vertical farm for salad greens. It costs $40m now, depreciated straight line to zero over 4 years.
  • Base case: 2,000 tonnes a year sold at $16,000 a tonne. Variable cost $6,000 a tonne. Fixed cash costs $6m a year.
  • Tax 25%. Discount rate 9%. No working capital or salvage in the base case.
  • Scenarios: best (25%) 2,400 t at $17,000; base (50%); worst (25%) 1,600 t at $14,000.
  • Option: at the end of year 1 the farm could be sold for $25m. Its book value then is $30m, so the loss on sale saves tax.
