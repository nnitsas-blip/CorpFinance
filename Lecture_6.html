Spot the mistakes

An analyst has built the cash flows for an automated packing robot at a pharmacy distributor. Five of the eleven lines are wrong. Click the lines you think are wrong, then check (slides 8 to 12, 23 and 30).

The facts. The robot costs $120m now and lasts 4 years, depreciated straight line to zero. Revenue $80m, $90m, $90m, $70m; cash costs 35% of revenue; tax 25%. Working capital is 10% of each year's revenue. It takes warehouse space the distributor could let for $6m a year. A $4m feasibility study was paid last year. The robot is funded with a $100m loan at 5%. The distributor's WACC is 9%.

Mini case: should we open a new gym?

Work the case from start to finish, the way the lecture worked the lab: sort the file, build the free cash flow, then decide. Check each step as you go; the solution for a step appears once you ask for it.

The analyst's file ($m, fictional)

  • A gym chain is deciding whether to open a new branch. Fit-out and equipment cost $60m now, depreciated straight line to zero over 3 years. The equipment can be sold for $6m at the end of year 3.
  • Membership revenue: $60m, $80m, $70m in years 1 to 3. Cash operating costs are 40% of revenue.
  • Tax 20%. WACC 10%. Working capital is 10% of each year's revenue, all recovered in year 3.

Also in the file:

    Step 1: sort the file

    Decide whether each item belongs in the NPV.

    Step 2: build the free cash flow

    Enter each figure in $m, costs and outflows as negative numbers. Leave a cell blank if nothing happens that year.

    Step 3: decide

    Use the free cash flows from step 2. NPV with =NPV(rate, years 1 to 3) + year 0, as in the previous lecture.
